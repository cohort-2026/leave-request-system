# SaaS Product Brief

## Leave / Employee Request Management System

The proposed product is a multi-tenant SaaS platform for organisations that need to manage employee requests, including leave applications. Each subscribed organisation should be able to use the same application while keeping its employees, policies, calendars and leave data separate from every other organisation.

Many organisations currently manage these requests through email, WhatsApp and other communication channels.

The platform should provide a consistent, configurable workflow for submitting requests, reviewing them and keeping track of decisions. Organisation administrators should be able to configure their own leave types, working calendars, public holidays, approval rules and employee onboarding process without requiring changes to the shared application.

Employees should have better visibility of requests they have submitted, while managers and HR users should be able to review and manage requests within their organisation. Users must only be able to access data permitted by their role and organisation membership.

The product should be designed for onboarding multiple organisations over time, with a platform administrator responsible for managing tenants and organisation administrators responsible for their own configuration. We are open to your team's recommendations on pricing, subscription tiers and future SaaS features, but tenant isolation, role-based access and organisation-specific configuration are core product requirements.

## SaaS Scope and Success Criteria

A single-client application would solve the leave process for one organisation. Its policies, organisation chart, calendars, integrations and risk profile could be shaped around that client's specific needs, with success measured by whether the working system matches the company's rules and is accepted by its stakeholders.

This product has a broader SaaS responsibility. It must solve leave management for many organisations whose policies, organisation charts, calendars, integrations and risk profiles may differ, without requiring the product to be rewritten for each customer. Configuration, permissions, workflows and integrations therefore need to be adaptable through the product itself, while tenant data and security boundaries remain isolated.

In the SaaS model, success means that an organisation unfamiliar to the project team can discover or be onboarded to the platform, configure its own leave process, subscribe to an appropriate plan, and continue using the product successfully without a project team living inside its business. This makes self-service setup, maintainability, secure multi-tenancy, clear administration and dependable support for different organisational contexts core product outcomes.

## Legal, Security, Data Protection and IP Considerations

A one-off client engagement would normally be governed by a services agreement, a statement of work and a data processing schedule. The client would remain the controller of its employees' personal data, while our team would process that data for the project. Hosting, security questionnaires and other operational decisions could be agreed directly with that client.

A SaaS platform serves many organisations that remain independent controllers. It therefore needs standardised, scalable legal and operational foundations, including:

- Published terms of service and a privacy policy.
- A data processing agreement that can be executed at scale.
- A maintained list of subprocessors.
- Clear incident notification commitments.
- A documented position on data residency and hosting locations.
- Controls that support South African POPIA obligations and any GDPR exposure created by EU employees or customers.

Customers will expect clear answers about encryption, access control, audit logs, retention and deletion, backups, availability and breach response. Security claims must reflect the controls we actually operate; claims such as “bank-grade security” should not be made unless they can be evidenced. SOC 2 or ISO 27001 may become future assurance goals, but the product should first establish sound security practices and auditable controls.

The ownership model also changes. In a client project, the contract may assign the deliverable to the client or provide exclusive use of its configuration. In SaaS, the company must retain ownership of the platform, source code, generic workflows, infrastructure and reusable product improvements, while granting customers a licence to use the service. Customer data and customer-specific configurations must remain appropriately protected and controlled by the customer. Any early custom-build agreement must preserve our right to reuse generic platform components so that the work can evolve into a product.
